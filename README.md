# SOPWITH GB

A Game Boy (DMG) homage to the 1984 PC game **Sopwith** by David L. Clark (BMB Compuscience).
It was written from scratch in C for GBDK-2020. It is not a port of the original source code, and all graphics are new.

## What's in it

- A side-scrolling world 1536 px wide, with hills, trees, your airfield on the far left and the enemy airfield on the far right.
- 16-direction flight with Sopwith-style controls. Pitch is relative to the pilot, so flying upside down reverses Up/Down, and you can roll the plane over.
- Throttle, fuel, stalls (the nose drops when you fly too slowly) and speed that changes as you climb or dive.
- Machine gun and bombs. You have limited fuel, ammunition and bombs.
- Land slowly and level on your own runway to refuel and re-arm. Landing anywhere else, or too fast, is a crash.
- 13 ground targets: factories, fuel tanks, tanks and the enemy hangar. Bullets wear them down, and one bomb destroys them.
- Enemy biplanes take off from the enemy base and hunt you. Shot planes spin down trailing smoke.
- A flock of birds that you can hit, or fly into.
- A HUD with a minimap of the whole world, gauges for fuel and ammo, and counts for bombs, lives and mission.
- Missions get harder each time: more enemies that are faster and more aggressive. Destroying every target gives a bonus and an extra plane.
- Sound: an engine drone whose pitch follows your speed, plus gunfire, the whistle of a falling bomb, explosions and hits.

## Controls

| Button | Action |
|---|---|
| Up / Down | Pitch nose up / down (relative to the pilot) |
| Left / Right | Throttle. The way you face is faster, the other way is slower. When you're stopped on the runway, the other way turns the plane around. |
| A | Machine gun (hold) |
| B | Drop bomb |
| Select | Flip (roll 180°) |
| Start | Start / pause |

**Takeoff:** hold the direction you face to open the throttle. Once you have speed, press Up.
**Turning around:** do a half loop (keep pulling Up) and then press Select to roll right side up. You can also just fly into the edge of the world.
**Landing:** approach your runway (far left), throttle back, and touch down at a shallow angle. Wait while stopped to refuel and re-arm.

## Building

You need [GBDK-2020](https://github.com/gbdk-2020/gbdk-2020) (tested with 4.x).

```sh
make GBDK_HOME=/path/to/gbdk
```

This produces `sopwith.gb`, a 32 KB ROM-only cartridge that runs on DMG/MGB/GBC and in any emulator.

Without make:

```sh
/path/to/gbdk/bin/lcc -Wf--opt-code-speed -Wm-yn"SOPWITH GB" -o sopwith.gb src/main.c src/assets.c
```

`src/assets.c` and `src/assets.h` are generated by `tools/gen_assets.py`, which draws the plane rotations, tiles, font and terrain. They're already included. Regenerate them with `make assets` (needs Python 3) if you change the art, the terrain seed or the target layout.

## Technical notes

- BG and sprites share tile data at 0x8000. Sprites are 8×16. Each 16×16 plane uses 2 hardware sprites, and 8 pre-rotated images plus hardware X/Y flips cover all 16 headings, upright and inverted.
- The world is a 192×16 tile map in WRAM. Columns stream into the 32-wide BG ring buffer during VBlank as the camera scrolls. Destroyed targets are rewritten in place.
- The minimap is drawn into a 12-tile RAM buffer and uploaded 3 tiles per frame during VBlank.
- The two hottest loops, moving projectiles and building OAM entries, are hand-written SM83 assembly. The rest is C tuned for SDCC. The game holds about 60 fps even in busy dogfights.
